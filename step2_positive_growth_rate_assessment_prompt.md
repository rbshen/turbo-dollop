# Step 2: Positive Growth Rate

**Ticker: {{TICKER}}**

You are assessing whether {{TICKER}} has a **projected** growth rate for the next 3–5 years that is positive and meaningful. This step is deliberately forward-looking — you are buying the future of the business, not its past. Historical growth rates alone are NOT a valid substitute for this assessment.

**Pass criterion:** Projected 3–5 year growth rate must be positive, and ideally meaningful (not just barely above zero), supported by a credible qualitative growth story.

---

## Step 1 — Gather Projected Growth Rates from Multiple Sources

Never rely on a single source. Collect projected 3–5 year growth rates (revenue and/or EPS, as available) from at least 3–4 independent platforms, for example:

- GuruFocus (Future 3–5 year growth rate)
- Finviz (Next 5 year EPS growth)
- Zacks (Expected 3–5 year growth rate)
- A comparable analyst-consensus or data platform of your choosing (e.g., Seeking Alpha, Yahoo Finance analyst estimates, TIKR, or company guidance)

| Source | Projected Growth % |
|---|---|
| Source 1 | ___% |
| Source 2 | ___% |
| Source 3 | ___% |
| Source 4 (optional) | ___% |
| **Average** | ___% |

`Average = Sum of all source projections / Number of sources`

If sources diverge significantly (e.g., a >10 percentage point spread), note this explicitly rather than silently averaging it away — wide disagreement is itself a data point.

## Step 2 — Assess the Projected Growth Rate

| Projected Growth Rate | Assessment |
|---|---|
| > 15% projected growth | ✅ Strong Pass — high growth business |
| 10–15% projected growth | ✅ Solid Pass — healthy grower |
| 5–10% projected growth | ⚠️ Pass — modest but acceptable |
| 0–5% projected growth | ⚠️ Borderline — very slow growth |
| Negative projected growth | ❌ Fail — do not proceed |

The higher the projected growth rate, the higher the intrinsic value that will be calculated downstream — so accuracy here matters enormously for the rest of the evaluation.

## Step 3 — Validate with Qualitative Growth Catalysts

Numbers alone are not enough — always ask **why** the business is expected to grow. Research the company's forward-looking narrative (via available research tools, recent earnings calls, investor presentations, or analyst commentary) and look for evidence of:

| Growth Catalyst | Example |
|---|---|
| Operating in a growth industry | Cybersecurity, AI, Healthcare |
| Expanding into new markets | Geographic or demographic expansion |
| New products or services pipeline | Innovation driving new revenue streams |
| Growing customer base | Network effects pulling in more users |
| Strategic acquisitions | Bolt-on growth adding revenue |
| Demographic/structural tailwinds | Aging population, reshoring, electrification, etc. |

If the projected numbers are strong but no clear reason for growth can be identified, treat that as a warning sign — numbers need a credible story behind them.

## Step 4 — Cross-Check Qualitative Against Quantitative

Ask: do the growth catalysts justify the projected growth rate from the data sources?

| Catalysts | Projection | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Strong | High | ✅ Confident Pass |
| Strong | Low | ⚠️ Conservative estimate — may still pass |
| Weak | High | 🚩 Projection may be overly optimistic |
| Weak | Low | ❌ Fail — no compelling growth case |

---

## Overall Assessment Framework

Answer the 4 governing questions explicitly:

1. **SOURCE CONSENSUS** — Do multiple platforms agree on a positive growth rate? → 3+ sources positive ✅ / Mixed or conflicting ⚠️ (average carefully) / All negative ❌ Fail
2. **MAGNITUDE** — Is the projected rate meaningful? → Above 10% ✅ / 5–10% ⚠️ / Below 5% or negative 🚩
3. **CATALYST** — Is there a credible reason to grow? → Clear and multiple ✅ / Vague or weak 🚩
4. **ALIGNMENT** — Do catalysts match the numbers? → Story fits data ✅ / Story contradicts data 🚩

| Result | Verdict |
|---|---|
| All 4 Green | ✅ Strong Pass — proceed to next step |
| 3 Green, 1 Yellow | ⚠️ Pass with monitoring |
| 2+ Red flags | 🚩 May Not Pass — reconsider |
| Majority Red | ❌ Fail — eliminate from watchlist |

---

**Notes for use:**
- Always label figures as **projected/forward-looking** — do not substitute historical CAGR for this step even if forward data is hard to find; if forward estimates are unavailable, state that explicitly as a limitation rather than filling the gap with trailing growth.
- Prefer revenue and EPS growth estimates specifically over vague "growth outlook" commentary.
- Note the estimate date/vintage for each source — projections more than 6-12 months old may be stale, especially after a recent earnings report or guidance revision.
- This prompt should be run identically for any ticker supplied; only the data and sources pulled change.
