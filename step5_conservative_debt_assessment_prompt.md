# Step 5: Conservative Debt

**Ticker: {{TICKER}}**

You are assessing whether {{TICKER}} carries a conservative, sustainable debt load. A great business with an unsustainable debt load can — and does — collapse. High debt is the single most common reason great-looking companies go bankrupt. This step is a bankruptcy filter, not a growth or quality check: a business can pass every other step and still be wiped out by excessive leverage.

Debt itself is not inherently bad — used wisely it can amplify returns. But excessive debt creates three dangers: rising interest rates causing interest costs to soar and profits to collapse; an economic downturn dropping revenue while debt obligations stay fixed; and refinancing risk, where debt comes due at the worst possible time. A debt-free company cannot go bankrupt — this step ensures {{TICKER}} is built to survive, not just built to grow.

**Pass criterion:** For standard (non-Bank, non-REIT, non-Property Developer) companies, ALL 3 of the following must pass:
1. Current Ratio ≥ 1
2. Debt/EBITDA Ratio ≤ 3
3. Debt Servicing Ratio < 30%

Sector-specific criteria apply instead for Banks, REITs, and Property Developers (see below).

## Standard Companies — The 3 Ratios

### Ratio 1 — Current Ratio

`Current Ratio = Current Assets / Current Liabilities`

Tells you whether the business can comfortably meet its short-term obligations using its short-term assets. Current Assets = cash, receivables, inventory due within 1 year. Current Liabilities = bills, payables, short-term debt due within 1 year.

| Current Ratio | Assessment |
|---|---|
| > 2.0 | ✅ Excellent — very strong short-term buffer |
| 1.5 – 2.0 | ✅ Good — comfortable coverage |
| 1.0 – 1.5 | ✅ Acceptable — meets minimum threshold |
| < 1.0 | ❌ Fail — liabilities exceed short-term assets |

**Deferred Revenue exception:** Some companies intentionally carry a lower Current Ratio because Deferred Revenue sits as a Current Liability (e.g., cash already collected for annual software subscriptions not yet delivered). This is not a real financial obligation — the company already has the cash. If a low Current Ratio for {{TICKER}} is driven primarily by deferred revenue, this is **not** a red flag. Always check what is actually driving Current Liabilities before judging the ratio.

### Ratio 2 — Debt/EBITDA Ratio

`Debt/EBITDA = (Short Term Debt + Long Term Debt) / EBITDA`

Tells you how many years of operating earnings it would take to fully repay all debt. Lower = debt repayable quickly = conservative and safe; higher = heavily leveraged = higher downturn risk.

| Debt/EBITDA | Assessment |
|---|---|
| ≤ 1.0 | ✅ Excellent — very low leverage |
| 1.0 – 2.0 | ✅ Good — manageable debt load |
| 2.0 – 3.0 | ✅ Acceptable — at the upper boundary |
| > 3.0 | ❌ Fail — debt load is too high |

For context: a Debt/EBITDA of 2.0 means the company could clear all debt in 2 years using its full EBITDA; a ratio of 5.0 means 5 years of earnings — dangerously high. ≤ 3 is treated as a hard ceiling; well below 3 is preferable.

Note: a simple "cash exceeds total debt" comparison is **not** one of the 3 official criteria — stick to the ratios defined here.

### Ratio 3 — Debt Servicing Ratio

`Debt Servicing Ratio = (Net Interest Expense / Cash Flow from Operations) × 100%`

Tells you what percentage of operating cash flow is consumed by interest payments alone. Low = light interest burden, plenty of cash for growth/dividends/reinvestment; high = vulnerable to rate rises or any dip in operating cash flow.

| Debt Servicing Ratio | Assessment |
|---|---|
| < 10% | ✅ Excellent — interest is barely a burden |
| 10 – 20% | ✅ Good — manageable interest load |
| 20 – 30% | ⚠️ Approaching the limit — monitor closely |
| ≥ 30% | ❌ Fail — too much cash diverted to interest |

Cash Flow from Operations is used as the denominator (rather than Net Income) because CFO is much harder to manipulate through accounting adjustments and reflects actual cash generated by the business — giving a truer picture of how burdensome interest payments really are.

## Sector Exceptions

### Banks

Banks use debt (deposits) as their core raw material to generate profit — high leverage is the business model, not mismanagement. Applying Debt/EBITDA or Debt Servicing Ratio to a bank produces meaningless results. Instead, check:

`Common Equity Tier 1 (CET1) Ratio > 10%` — the global regulatory standard for bank financial strength, measuring core equity capital as a percentage of risk-weighted assets.

| CET1 Ratio | Assessment |
|---|---|
| > 13% | ✅ Excellent — very well capitalised |
| 11 – 13% | ✅ Good — solidly above threshold |
| 10 – 11% | ✅ Acceptable — meets minimum |
| < 10% | ❌ Fail — undercapitalised, higher risk |

### REITs / Property Developers

REITs are legally required to distribute the majority of income as dividends, so they structurally carry more debt to fund property acquisitions. Applying standard debt ratios would unfairly disqualify virtually every REIT. Instead, check:

`Gearing Ratio = Total Debt / Total Assets ≤ 45%`

| Gearing Ratio | Assessment |
|---|---|
| < 30% | ✅ Excellent — conservatively structured |
| 30 – 40% | ✅ Good — healthy leverage for a REIT |
| 40 – 45% | ⚠️ Approaching the regulatory limit |
| > 45% | ❌ Fail — over-leveraged for a REIT |

| ST Debt + LT Debt | Balance sheet (directly) |

| CET1 Ratio (Banks) | Company annual report / investor relations disclosures |
| Gearing Ratio (REITs) | Company financial statements / REIT disclosures |

## Overall Assessment Framework

**For standard companies**, answer 3 questions:

1. **CURRENT RATIO ≥ 1?** → Yes, and deferred-revenue check done ✅ / Just below 1 — investigate why ⚠️ / Clearly below 1 ❌ Fail
2. **DEBT/EBITDA ≤ 3?** → ≤ 2, strong ✅ / 2–3, acceptable ✅ / > 3 ❌ Fail
3. **DEBT SERVICING RATIO < 30%?** → < 20%, comfortable ✅ / 20–30%, watch carefully ⚠️ / ≥ 30% ❌ Fail

| Result | Verdict |
|---|---|
| All 3 Green | ✅ Strong Pass — financially resilient |
| 2 Green, 1 Yellow | ⚠️ Pass — monitor the weak ratio |
| Any ratio at or beyond hard limit | ❌ Fail — too much financial risk |
| Sector exception applies | 🔄 Apply CET1 (Banks) or Gearing (REITs) instead |
