# Financials

The Financials card looks at whether a company's core financial results —
the money it brings in, keeps, and generates in cash — are growing in a
healthy, sustainable way. It checks five things together:

- **Revenue** — the total sales the company generates. If revenue isn't
  growing, the underlying business isn't growing either, no matter what
  else looks good.
- **Net Income** — the company's bottom-line profit after all expenses,
  taxes, and one-off items. This is the most commonly cited profit figure,
  but it's also the easiest to distort with one-time gains or charges, so
  it's checked alongside the other four rather than on its own.
- **Cash From Operations (CFO)** — the actual cash the business generated
  from running its operations, separate from accounting profit. A company
  can report a profit on paper while not generating real cash, so this is
  treated as more trustworthy than Net Income alone.
- **Margins** — gross margin and net margin, i.e. how much of each dollar
  of revenue the company actually keeps after costs. Stable or improving
  margins suggest pricing power and cost discipline; margins that are
  steadily eroding are a warning sign even if revenue is still growing.
- **Free Cash Flow (FCF)** — cash left over after the company pays for its
  own capital investments (equipment, facilities, etc.). This is checked
  mainly for whether it stays positive, since a sustained multi-year run
  of cash burn is a real bankruptcy-risk signal.

## Why current profitability matters, not just the trend

It's not enough for these figures to be trending in the right direction —
Revenue, Net Income, and CFO are also checked for whether they're
currently positive. A company whose losses are merely getting smaller
year over year, but still hasn't turned a profit, is not treated the same
as one that has genuinely turned the corner into profitability. A
temporary dip that has since fully recovered is tolerated; a business that
has never actually been profitable is not.

## Some company types skip the cash-flow checks

Cash From Operations and Free Cash Flow don't mean the same thing for
every kind of business. For **Banks, Insurance companies, Property
Developers, and Commodity companies**, these two checks are skipped, and
the company is judged on Revenue, Net Income, and Margins instead (with
those three carrying proportionally more weight to make up for it). This
isn't a loophole — it reflects that "cash from operations" is measured and
reported very differently for a bank taking deposits or an insurer holding
long-duration reserves than it is for a typical operating company. See
[Company type variations](company-type-variations.md) for more.

## What the verdict means

Financials produces a single score out of 100 and a verdict — **Fail**,
**Pass**, or **Strong Pass**. Broadly:

- A **Fail** means one or more of the core figures are weak, still
  unprofitable, or trending in a genuinely troubling direction.
- A **Pass** means the company's revenue, profit, and cash generation are
  healthy overall, even if not every single figure is perfect.
- A **Strong Pass** means all of the checked figures are consistently
  strong across the whole reporting window.

If the underlying figures simply aren't available for a company — a data
gap, not a real weakness — Financials reports as having **insufficient
data** rather than fabricating a Fail. See the [Glossary](glossary.md) for
how every verdict label is defined.
